5. Voice, feature packs, and features with legal restrictions where you live

5.1 Voice, and recording other people

Walkie-talkie voice is live audio to your convoy and, if you switch proximity voice on, to riders within range who have switched it on too. Convoy does not record it. You must not record it either without the consent of everyone speaking — several jurisdictions require the consent of all parties, not just one. Where you are decides whether a recording is lawful; Convoy makes the conversation possible and knowing the rule is on you. Do not use voice to relay enforcement locations to a driver where §5.3 applies.

Proximity voice is off until you turn it on, and Invisible mode silences it. A rider you have blocked can neither hear you nor be heard by you.

Displaying or being warned about the location of speed cameras, mobile enforcement or police is restricted or unlawful in a number of countries, including — among others — Germany, France, Switzerland and Austria, with rules that vary by country as to whether the driver may be warned at all. Where you are subject to such a rule you must not use these layers, and you must not relay what they show to a driver. Convoy does not pair with radar detectors.

5.4 Police scanner audio (Police scanners pack)

Convoy links to publicly available scanner feeds. Listening to public-safety radio in a moving vehicle is restricted or unlawful in several US states — including, among others, Florida, Indiana, Kentucky, Minnesota, New York and New Jersey — and using what you hear to commit or escape an offence is a separate and more serious offence almost everywhere. In some countries reception itself requires a licence. Convoy does not transmit on any radio service and does not decode encrypted traffic.

5.5 Timed runs (Performance timers pack)

The timers measure acceleration and timed distances from GPS. Using them on a public road is street racing, which is illegal everywhere, and a breach of §4. They are for closed courses and private land with permission, and timing a run is a passenger’s job — the driver drives.

5.6 Aircraft data

Aircraft positions come from public ADS-B feeds. They are supplied for interest and situational awareness. They are not for any aeronautical, operational, navigational, or surveillance purpose, and must not be used to identify, track, or approach any aircraft or the people on it.

6. Accuracy — what the data is and is not

Convoy draws public map data and the positions other riders report. All of it is indicative, incomplete, and often out of date. Specifically:

  • Routes, distances and arrival times are estimates and may direct you down roads that are closed, restricted, private, unsuitable for your vehicle, or unsafe. The sign on the road always wins.
  • Other riders’ positions are delayed, interpolated, and may be simulated or server-generated where the map would otherwise be empty.
  • The global layer is deliberately delayed by about five minutes and must never be read as a live position.

Never rely on Convoy for anything safety-critical, time-critical, or where being wrong has a real consequence. Convoy is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
